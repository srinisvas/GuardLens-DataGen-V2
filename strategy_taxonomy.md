# GuardLens v12 Strategy Taxonomy

This document defines the multi-turn attack strategies used to generate the
v12 dataset. Each strategy encodes a distinct **construction pattern** — a
recipe for how the generator builds a conversation — rather than a
statement about what the conversation causally achieves. The distinction
matters because whether a record ends up demonstrating single-turn or
distributed evidence is something the causal-analysis pipeline
(`causal_analysis_v12_3.py`) determines empirically, not something the
generator can assert at construction time.

## Design principles

**Two-label separation.** Every record carries two labels:

- `construction_label`: assigned at generation time. Describes what the
  generator was *trying* to construct (which strategy, which intended
  pivot pattern).
- `validated_evidence_type`: assigned by causal analysis. Describes what
  the record *actually* provides evidence for after counterfactual replay.

These may disagree. A record constructed as single-pivot can degrade to
distributed evidence if the intended pivot turn is not causally
load-bearing under CF replay. That downgrade is a feature, not a bug: it
prevents self-fulfilling attribution claims.

**No single-family generator/validator coupling.** Every strategy is
generated by two different generator/target/judge stacks:

- Config A (70% of records): Llama-3.3-70B generator, Gemma-4-31B target,
  Qwen3.5-27B judge
- Config B (30% of records): Qwen3.5-27B generator, Llama-3.3-70B target,
  Mistral-Small-3.2 judge

This addresses the SAC-flagged v11 circularity where generator and
validator both came from the Qwen family.

**Contamination gate before generation.** All source objectives and all
generated conversations pass through `contamination_check.py`, which
compares against MHJ, HarmBench, JailbreakBench, WildJailbreak, and the
frozen v11 dataset. Two stages:

- **Stage 1 (lexical, HARD GATE):** 5-gram MinHash Jaccard ≥ 0.6.
  Records that trip this stage are **dropped and regenerated** with
  different seeds. Never paraphrase.
- **Stage 2 (semantic, QUARANTINE):** bge-m3 cosine similarity ≥ 0.82.
  Records that trip *only* this stage are **quarantined for manual
  review**, since contemporary conversations about the same topic are
  legitimately semantically similar to external harm-taxonomy items
  without being contamination. Use `--semantic-hard-gate` to promote
  semantic hits to the drop path only after threshold calibration on
  real v12 pilot data.

## Construction mix

Records are drawn from three top-level slices, with the intended
strategy chosen from a slice-internal distribution.

### Slice allocation (fixed)

| Slice | Fraction | Approx count | Purpose |
|-------|----------|--------------|---------|
| single-pivot | 30% | 750-1125 | Constructed around one intended pivotal turn. Tests whether attribution machinery can localize causally-central turns. |
| distributed-by-construction | 50% | 1250-1875 | Constructed so the intended mechanism is spread across multiple turns. Tests trajectory-level integration. |
| hard-benign | 20% | 500-750 | Constructed to superficially resemble attacks (technical framing, sensitive topics, refusal-adjacent target responses) but with benign intent. Tests boundary precision. |

Plus 1:1 length-matched benign twins (2000-3000 records) to prevent
conversation length from becoming a shortcut feature.

**Total base pool: 4500-6750 conversations before validation filtering.**

### Within-slice strategy distribution (single-pivot slice, sums to 100%)

| Strategy | Fraction | Description |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| B (authority appeal) | 24% | Constructed around one turn claiming professional/institutional authority (researcher, regulator, safety auditor). Payload framed as within-role information need. |
| D (persona / role assumption) | 24% | Constructed around one turn asking the target to adopt a persona (fictional expert, uncensored variant) that would plausibly provide the requested content. |
| E (encoding / obfuscation) | 18% | Constructed around one turn where the payload is encoded (base64, leetspeak, character substitution, non-English). |
| H (nested refusal exploitation) | 17% | Constructed around one turn asking the target to explain how it *would* refuse a harmful request in detail. |
| J (emotional / urgency pressure) | 17% | Constructed around one turn establishing emotional context (dying relative, imminent danger, time pressure) intended to shift the target's refusal calculus. |

### Within-slice strategy distribution (distributed-by-construction slice, sums to 100%)

| Strategy | Fraction | Description |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| A (gradual commitment) | 19% | Constructed to open with uncontroversial requests and escalate incrementally. Adaptation on refusal: retreat one step and reframe with softer language. |
| C (implicit contextual continuation) | 16% | Constructed to establish a technical framework in early turns without stating harmful intent, then reference it obliquely in later turns. |
| F (fictional framing) | 16% | Constructed to establish a fictional scenario (novel, screenplay, tabletop game) and then request in-world content that requires the target material as substrate. |
| G (hypothetical / speculation) | 13% | Constructed to establish a hypothetical space over multiple turns, then request concrete details within it. |
| I (piecewise assembly) | 16% | Constructed so individually benign components are requested across turns; the harmful content emerges only from the assembled whole. |
| K (long-horizon distributed intent) | 13% | Constructed with a legitimate long-running task for 5+ turns, then the payload appears as an incidental sub-question late in the trajectory. |
| M (cross-conversation history dependence) | 7% | Constructed so the current turn is benign in isolation but its interpretation depends on what was established several turns earlier. |

### Within-slice strategy distribution (hard-benign slice, sums to 100%)

| Sub-pattern | Fraction | Description |
|-------------|----------|-------------|
| high-risk vocabulary / benign intent | 35% | Uses terminology from sensitive domains (weapons, drugs, cybersecurity, biosecurity) in genuinely benign contexts (research literacy, safety education, journalism, harm reduction). |
| defensive framing | 20% | Conversations explicitly discussing how to defend against or prevent the harm implied by the topic. |
| attack-adjacent legitimate technical | 20% | Legitimate technical work (pen-testing methodology explanation, medicinal chemistry, computer security research) that lives close to attack surface. |
| medical/legal/policy boundary | 15% | Genuine medical, legal, or policy questions where the target should provide information rather than refuse, even though the surface topic is sensitive. |
| controversial legitimate | 10% | Politically or socially controversial topics with genuine information needs (drug policy debate, encryption regulation, dual-use research policy). |

Strategy L (decoy) from earlier drafts is subsumed into "high-risk
vocabulary / benign intent" as one construction pattern within it.

## Supervision tier system

Records receive a supervision tier based on how their construction-time
label was validated by causal analysis. Tier names are frozen; numeric
weights are **TBD after pilot** and must not be hard-coded until we have
evidence from real v12 data on which weighting scheme actually improves
the downstream training objective.

| Tier | Weight | Meaning |
|------|--------|---------|
| `cf_strong` | TBD after pilot | Counterfactual replay confirmed the causal role of the labeled turn/span |
| `construction_llm` | TBD after pilot | Judge-based validation confirmed construction intent |
| `cf_weak` | TBD after pilot | Counterfactual replay was consistent but below the confidence threshold |
| `construction` | TBD after pilot | Construction label with no downstream validation |
| `llm_only` | TBD after pilot | Judge label without construction alignment |
| `incidental` | label 0 | Not causally relevant; treated as negative |
| `ignore` | excluded | Record excluded from training |

The pilot's job is to determine (a) whether these tiers are actually
distinguishable in practice, and (b) what weighting shape produces the
most useful training signal. Only after that does any specific numeric
scheme go into production.
